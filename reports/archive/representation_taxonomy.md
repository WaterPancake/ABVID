# Representation taxonomy

Phase 2, written after [physical_signal_model.md](physical_signal_model.md) and before the domain-shift analysis. Paper IDs and source paths are in [literature_review.md](literature_review.md#source-inventory). Claims labelled **author** report the paper; **interpretation** describes our analysis. “Unknown” means that the cited experiment does not establish the property.

## Comparison in physical terms

| Representation and source | Preserved information | Discarded or suppressed information | Intended invariance and physical limits |
|---|---|---|---|
| Harmonic-order powers: Altmann et al. [P05, §§3.2–3.5] | Relative energy at identified engine harmonics. | Absolute phase, scalar level and uniform frequency scaling; non-series components are omitted. | **Author:** first-order gain/frequency-scale invariance. Relative amplitudes still change with RPM, direction, frequency-dependent propagation and SNR. This is not complete distance or engine-state invariance. [P05, §3.4, footnote 4; §5] |
| Harmonic line association: Wu–Mendel [P13, chapter 3, §2.2; P08, §III-C; P10, §2.3] | Harmonic magnitudes 2–12, outlining a low-frequency spectral envelope. P13 normalizes channel energy and averages three channels. | Most broadband content and phase; detailed time evolution inside the short window is not retained. | **Interpretation:** indexing by harmonics helps accommodate frequency movement but does not stabilize a changing harmonic balance. Fuzzy uncertainty models tolerate distributions of features; they do not prove physical-factor invariance. [P13, chapter 3, §§2.2–3] |
| Harmonic plus selected spectral bins: Guo et al. [P12, §§3–5] | Harmonic structure plus discriminative non-harmonic frequency components selected by mutual information. | Unselected frequencies and phase; low-frequency truncation limits bandwidth. | **Author:** complementary engine/body and exterior-noise information; key-bin stability can change with velocity/road condition. **Interpretation:** a selected bin is not a verified isolated tire or aerodynamic source. [P12, §§3–4, 7] |
| Time-domain harmonics' amplitudes (TDHA): William–Hoffman [P09, §§III–IV] | Fifty fitted harmonic amplitudes, event energy and estimated strongest frequency, using a short waveform template. | Phase and much non-harmonic content; the template reduces samples used for fitting. | **Author:** the fitted fundamental is an approximation grid derived from the strongest component, not necessarily the physical engine fundamental. **Interpretation:** energy and strongest frequency explicitly retain range/state information; this is not an RPM- or gain-invariant vector by construction. [P09, §§III-B, IV, VI] |
| Broad spectral descriptors: Wieczorkowska et al. [P15, §3] | Spectrum envelope, energy, centroid, spread, rolloff, F0 estimates, selected band ratios, bandwidths and zero-crossing rate. | Most phase and subframe waveform detail. Energy-normalized ratios suppress common scaling. | **Interpretation:** fixed bands can respond to RPM, speed, sensor filtering and environment; normalization cannot remove differential spectral coloration. The authors leave explicit speed/acceleration/tire analysis and Doppler handling to later work. [P15, §§3, 7] |
| Wavelet-packet summaries: Göksu [P11, §§III–V] | Energy or entropy of time-frequency subbands, with 32 final nodes in the selected decomposition. | Summarizing each subband discards the exact coefficient ordering; it is not the full time-frequency signal. | **Author:** a vehicle-specific signature across 800–3000 r/min sweeps. **Interpretation:** empirical tolerance on four cars, not a mathematical RPM invariance or a new-sensor result. [P11, §II; §III-B, PDF p.4; Table 1] |
| Mel/MFCC and PCEN: Becker et al. [P03, §§3.1–3.4] | Mel-band energy patterns; MFCC transforms log-band energies; PCEN retains normalized band activity. | Waveform phase and fine within-band structure. PCEN suppresses stationary background and foreground loudness variation. | **Author:** adaptive gain control and compression address noise/loudness variation. **Interpretation:** this is not harmonic-order normalization and supplies no established RPM, speed, or calibrated-microphone invariance. [P03, §§3.2, 4.1, 5.2] |
| Modulation-spectrum features: Becker et al. [P03, §§3.3, 3.5] | Rates of change in PCEN/MFCC channels, averaged over modulation windows. | Absolute modulation phase and exact placement/order of the averaged modulation windows. | **Author:** aimed at dynamic engine/acceleration characteristics and heterogeneous backgrounds. **Interpretation:** retaining modulation can help distinguish source dynamics, but time scaling changes modulation frequencies; speed invariance is unknown. [P03, §§1, 3.3, 5.2] |
| Task-trained embeddings: Becker et al. [P03, §§3.6–3.7, 4.2] | A learned similarity representation for heavy-goods/personal-car or diesel/petrol pairs. | The network is optimized to compress distinctions irrelevant to these supplied labels; exactly which physical details it loses is unknown. | **Author:** contrastive same-class/different-class training. **Interpretation:** same-class pairing is not matched recording of the same source across known nuisance interventions, and t-SNE separation is not an independent domain-shift test. [P03, §§3.6, 4.3, 5.1–5.2] |
| Pretrained audio representation: BEATs [P07, §§3–4] | Contextual representations of Mel-filterbank patches, trained by masked discrete-label prediction and task-specific fine-tuning. | Waveform phase is absent from the input; semantic token training is intended to discard redundant low-level detail. Which vehicle-specific cues survive is unknown. | **Author:** semantic abstraction and qualitative robustness to added RIR/noise. **Interpretation:** the ten-example tokenizer visualization does not establish vehicle-model, RPM, speed, distance or new-device invariance. [P07, §§4.1–4.2, 4.5; Fig.4, PDF p.11] |

The preserved/discarded entries follow the stated computations; they are **representation-level interpretations**, not newly measured invariance results. A representation may retain class information and nuisance information simultaneously. [P05, §3.4; P12, §4; P03, §3; P07, §§3.3, 4.5]

|---|---|---|---|---|

[P05, §5] | Engine and track series are coupled by gears; selecting one does not remove the other. [P04, §§5.1–5.2] | Scalar-level normalization helps, but lower SNR and frequency-dependent propagation remain. [P05, §3.4] | Terrain-dependent feature distributions are observed. [P13, chapter 3, §2.3] |
| TDHA | Strongest frequency is explicitly retained; actual engine F0 is not the fitted F0. [P09, §§III–IV, VI] | Variety of passes is represented; independent held-out-speed tests are unknown. [P09, §V] | Energy retained; varied-range passes and station transfer tested. No distance-only effect is isolated. [P09, §§IV–V] | Separate-station test exists, but station also changes geometry/observation side. [P09, §V, Fig.8] |
| Spectral/MFCC features | Fixed spectral coordinates retain shifts; no explicit RPM invariance established. [P15, §3; P03, §§3–5] | Running speed discussed; on-road speed uncontrolled in P15. [P15, §§2.2–2.3, 5.3] | Some features normalized, others retain absolute level. A held-out-distance test is unknown. [P15, §3] | New-location/device isolation unknown in the reported classification protocols. [P15, §§5–6; P03, §§4–5] |
| Wavelet-packet entropy | Repeated known-range RPM sweeps tested. Extrapolation outside that range unknown. [P11, §§II, IV] | Road-speed variation unknown; source recordings use a fixed nearby position. [P11, §II] | Fixed 1 m geometry; distance robustness unknown. ## Evaluation evidence that survives the comparison

**Author evidence: real → real, individual recorded vehicles.** TDHA reports 90.38% event accuracy with random events, 82.10% with separate stations, and 82.77% with separate runs at approximately 5 Hz representation resolution. Separate-run temporal voting reaches 89.67%. The paper explicitly says one physical vehicle represents each class. Thus its stronger split evidence remains exemplar recognition. [P09, §V, footnote 5, Tables IV–V, PDF pp.8–11]

**Author evidence: real → real, nominal five vehicle-type labels.** Guo et al. randomly choose three of six whole runs per label for training and use the other three for testing, repeating ten times. Their harmonic baseline is 73.44% and modified Bayesian fusion 84.24% mean accuracy. Number of independent physical vehicles per type is unknown; this is not demonstrated new-exemplar model recognition. Speed stability, more complex data and new deployments remain future work. [P12, §§6–7, Tables 1–2; Table 2 checked on PDF p.12]

**Author evidence: real → real, broad civilian categories.** Wieczorkowska et al. keep vehicle segments grouped for the main on-road cross-validation, and use leave-one-vehicle-out across nine dyno vehicles for car/van and diesel/gasoline tasks. Separate three-class comparisons use random data splits. Dyno and road experiments are separate classification studies, not a road-trained → dyno-tested transfer result. [P15, §§3.1, 5.1–5.3, 6.1]

**Author evidence: real → real, broad category/engine attribute.** Becker et al. use a 65/15/20 training/validation/test allocation over repeated experiments they call 20-fold cross-validation. Recordings come from different vehicles, with diverse urban sites and supplemental web videos. The paper does not establish a held-out site or held-out device. [P03, §§4.2–5.1]

**Author evidence: general audio transfer, not a vehicle experiment.** BEATs evaluates AudioSet event labels, ESC-50 environmental classes, speech commands and speech emotions. Pretraining across datasets followed by target-task fine-tuning is not equivalent to source-only cross-domain vehicle evaluation. Its 98.1% ESC-50 result and 50.6% AudioSet ensemble mAP concern different labels, metrics and protocols from the vehicle results. [P07, §§4.1–4.6, Tables 1 and 3]

## Supplementary classifier papers in the collection

P08 (Wu–Mendel 2007) and P10 (Wu–Mendel binary classifier paper) were read after the five requested Phase 2 core papers. Both use ACIDS/HLA and fuzzy/Bayesian classifiers. They add protocol evidence rather than a new acoustic representation: random prototype folds and leave-two-runs-out in P08; leave-one-run-out and a sponsor-scored blind set in P10. The reported development test sets also participate in choosing model parameters/stopping; a separate untouched selection-validation layer is unknown. This is **our methodological concern**, not an author admission of leakage. [P08, §VI and pseudocode; P10, §4, PDF p.10]

**Author-acknowledged limits.** P08 asks when increased fuzzy-model complexity improves generalizability/robustness and how to quantify relevant uncertainty. P10 leaves multicategory/multiterrain extension for later work. These are broader-category studies; their hierarchical terminology does not establish fine-grained model recognition. [P08, §VII; P10, §5]

**Phase 3 working model.** We now distinguish three mechanisms: change the waveform distribution, change which signal details the representation preserves, or tolerate/alignment-correct the feature distribution in the classifier. Higher accuracy on known acquisition conditions does not establish which mechanism will repair an unseen domain. The next phase asks which physical mismatches each transfer experiment actually changes. [Review synthesis of P05, §3.4; P12, §§4–7; P03, §§3–5; P07, §§3–4]
